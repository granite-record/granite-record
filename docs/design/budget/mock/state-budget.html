Two Bills, Two Years

The state budget covers two fiscal years at once. The one in force now made appropriations “for the expenses of certain departments of the state for fiscal years ending June 30, 2026 and June 30, 2027”, in the words of its title, and took effect on July 1, 2025. (HB 1 of 2025, title and 140:18)

It comes as two bills. The constitution limits a budget bill to the operating and capital expenses of the executive, legislative and judicial branches: no section or footnote of one may establish, amend or repeal any other statutory law. (Part Second, Article 18-a) The statutes then make the changes to the law a document of their own, the trailer bill, separate from the budget and not a budget bill. (RSA 9:2-a) So HB 1 carries the amounts, and HB 2 the changes to the law that go with them. Both were introduced in the House. The constitution says all money bills shall originate there, and that the Senate may propose or concur with amendments. (Part Second, Article 18)

The operating budget

What it holds
The amounts each department may spend, the footnotes that govern them, and the estimates of revenue.
Became

HB 2

“Relative to state fees, funds, revenues, and expenditures”: the changes to the law. Its analysis lists 247.
Became

HB 1 and HB 2 have been the budget's two numbers in every term since 1993–1994. (This site's bill notes) Buildings and other capital projects are in a third bill, HB 25, described below.

The summary does not define “actuals”. Nor does it split the money by fund, so it does not say how much is general fund and how much federal.

The Capital Budget

Buildings, equipment and other capital projects are in a third bill. HB 25 has been titled “making appropriations for capital improvements” in every budget year since 1999, and in 2025 it went to the House Public Works and Highways committee and the Senate Capital Budget committee. Governor Ayotte signed it on July 15, 2025, as chapter 159.

Its section 1 appropriates $137.7 million of state money to 16 departments and agencies, and section 2 a further $10,000,000 from highway funds for projects of the Department of Transportation. (HB 25 of 2025, 159:1 and 159:2) The Fish and Game Department's three projects, $2,379,000, are each printed “Less Other Funds (Fish and Game Fund)”, “Net State 0”. (159:1, VI)

The operating budget may not use bonded debt to fund operating appropriations (RSA 9:8-b). The capital budget is funded by borrowing: “to provide funds for the total of the appropriations of state funds made in sections 1 and 2”, the act authorizes the state treasurer to borrow up to $171,060,895, and gives the source of funds as general fund $137,678,945, highway funds $10,000,000 and other funds $23,381,950. (HB 25 of 2025, 159:7) The other funds are the amounts section 1 prints as “Less Other Funds”: $19,700,000 from a revolving loan fund, $2,379,000 from the Fish and Game Fund and $1,302,950 from the Plate Fund.

It has a calendar of its own. Departments send their capital requests to the commissioner of administrative services by May 1 before the session; the commissioner sends the Governor a summary by May 31; the Governor holds public hearings on them by June 30. (RSA 9:3-a) For the next budget, the Department of Administrative Services presented its own requests, $143.4 million in all, at the Governor's capital budget hearing on June 16, 2026. (DAS presentation) Its slide of past capital appropriations gives $15,400,000 for 2025, the figure HB 25 printed for the department (paragraph I).

How the Budget Is Made

RSA 9 sets the calendar, and the docket of 2025 shows it happening.

  1. Departments ask. Every department sends the commissioner of administrative services its request for each year of the coming biennium. (RSA 9:4)

    By October 1 of the year before

  2. Income is estimated. The commissioner estimates the state's total income for each year of the biennium, source by source. (RSA 9:5)

    By October 1 of the year before

  3. A tentative budget. The commissioner puts the requests and the estimates together, and the director of the budget submits them to the Governor. (RSA 9:6)

    By November 1

  4. The Governor proposes. The Governor sends the legislature a budget and a trailer bill. The proposals are the Governor's own judgment. (RSA 9:2, 9:2-a, 9:8) In 2025 HB 1 and HB 2 were introduced on February 20 and referred to House Finance.

    By February 15

  5. House Finance. A public hearing on March 12, 2025; on April 3 the committee recommended both bills, amended, by 14 votes to 11.

    March and April 2025

  6. The House. On April 10 it passed HB 1 by 192 to 183 and HB 2 by 185 to 175. That was HB 2's 26th roll call of the day; most of the others were on amendments.

    April 10, 2025

  7. The Senate. Senate Finance held its hearing on May 6 and recommended both, amended, by 6 to 2. On June 5 the Senate passed HB 1 by 15 to 9 and HB 2 by 14 to 10.

    May and June 2025

  8. A committee of conference. The House did not accept the Senate's amendments and asked for a committee of conference; five representatives and three senators met on six days from June 12 to June 19.

    June 2025

  9. Both chambers vote on its report. On June 26 the House first rejected the HB 1 report by 182 to 183, reconsidered, and adopted it by 185 to 180; it adopted the HB 2 report by 184 to 183. The Senate adopted both by 16 to 8.

    June 26, 2025

  10. The Governor signs. Governor Ayotte signed both on June 27, 2025, as chapters 140 and 141, in force July 1.

    June 27, 2025

One rule binds the result. “The general court shall not adopt an operating budget for any fund, or any other legislation which provides for appropriations, which exceed the state's total estimated revenues from existing, increased, or new taxes, fees, operating income and other sources.” The operating budget may not use bonded debt to fund operating appropriations, and projected lapses may be counted in judging whether the rule is met. (RSA 9:8-b)

Now Under Way: The 2028–2029 Budget

Requests, not a budget. What follows is what the agencies have asked for. The Governor's budget, and then the legislature, decide what is appropriated.

The next budget, for fiscal 2028 and 2029, is being prepared now. The Department of Administrative Services' summary of the agencies' submissions, dated September 28, 2026, totals $9,093.6 million requested for fiscal 2028 and $9,318.0 million for fiscal 2029, in total funds, against $8,277.9 million adjusted authorized for fiscal 2027. (DAS summary, version 2028B01)

The summary shows each request in two columns, “efficiency” and “prioritized needs”, which add up to it. For fiscal 2028 they total $8,795.3 million and $298.3 million; for fiscal 2029, $8,988.6 million and $329.4 million. The statutes define the efficiency expenditure request as “the cost of providing the services authorized and funded in the preceding biennium, considering and incorporating changes in the population and other factors outside the control of the department…”, and ask it to identify “special or problematic needs”, which “are expected to be few and prioritized”. (RSA 9:4, II and IV(f)) The summary does not say whether its “prioritized needs” column is those.

The totals include the Legislative Branch, which does not submit a formal budget at this stage; its figures for fiscal 2028 and 2029 repeat its fiscal 2027 amount, as the summary's own note says.
